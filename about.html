About us

Gokuldas Tejpal Hospital has provided public healthcare in Mumbai since 1874. Its historic Gothic-style campus now also supports Government Medical College, Mumbai, which opened in 2024 with MBBS education. Together, the hospital and college carry forward a long tradition of public service while preparing a new generation of compassionate healthcare professionals.

From the Dean

It is a privilege to serve at an institution with a longstanding commitment to the health of Mumbai. The opening of Government Medical College begins an important new chapter, bringing medical education into a hospital environment shaped by generations of public service.

We want our students to become confident clinicians who also listen carefully, act with empathy, and uphold high ethical standards. Their education combines strong medical foundations with the practical insight gained through caring for patients.

Our first MBBS batch has completed its inaugural year, marking a significant step in the college's development. We continue to build on that experience and prepare a supportive academic environment for the students who join us next.

We aspire to make Government Medical College, Mumbai a place where excellent teaching, research, and patient care advance together. An inclusive campus and a culture of lifelong learning will help students and staff continue to grow professionally.

I extend my appreciation to the faculty and staff whose dedication helped establish the college. Together with our community, we can build an institution that serves patients and supports generations of future doctors.

Historical Foundation and Development

The hospital's origins lie in a nineteenth-century effort to create a place of care for local residents. An early proposal, made in 1865, could not proceed as planned when its funding arrangement fell through.

Administrator Arthur Crawford helped revive the project by securing the required philanthropic support from Gokuldas Tejpal, together with government participation. The foundation stone was laid on 10 May 1870, beginning construction of the hospital.

Completed in 1874, the building was designed in the English Gothic style by Colonel Fuller. Its blue basalt exterior, Kurla stone arches, and carefully finished interior details reflect the craftsmanship of the period.

The hospital was formally established in 1875. It has since remained a Government of Maharashtra institution, continuing its public service and providing care to people from across the city.

Mission and Educational Philosophy

Government Medical College, Mumbai is dedicated to delivering rigorous medical education through effective, evolving teaching methods and a strong academic foundation. The college aims to prepare skilled doctors who can take leadership in their specialties and respond to the health needs of Mumbai and communities beyond.

As a public institution, it pairs accessible education with high academic expectations. Students are encouraged to develop clinical competence alongside compassion, integrity, and a clear commitment to the people they serve.

The college also values research as an essential part of learning and better healthcare. Faculty and students are encouraged to advance medical knowledge through inquiry and collaboration with other institutions.

Vision and Aspirations

The college's vision is to grow into a leading institution for medical education, research, and healthcare. It seeks to graduate knowledgeable, compassionate, and socially responsible physicians prepared to deliver quality care in Mumbai and beyond.

By bringing academic excellence together with community service and ethical practice, Government Medical College, Mumbai aims to contribute to stronger health outcomes across the region. Lifelong learning and professional growth are central to that ambition.

Legacy and Impact

For more than 150 years, Gokuldas Tejpal Hospital has been an important source of public healthcare in South Mumbai. Its commitment to serving patients regardless of means continues the philanthropic purpose behind its founding.

The hospital's Gothic architecture and use of local materials preserve a visible record of the vision and cooperation that brought it to life. The historic campus remains an active part of the city's healthcare infrastructure.

As medical needs change, the institution continues to adapt while holding to its service mission. Its close relationship with medical education gives students meaningful clinical experience with diverse patients and helps them develop both professional skill and compassion.
